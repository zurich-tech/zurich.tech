## Selection

Include concrete releases, technical results, infrastructure and useful opportunities with a substantive Zurich connection.
Require a locally rooted company or project, or a development with a specific local consequence.
Incidental participation by a Zurich team in a global product update is not sufficient.
Separate routine event listings from news candidates.
Keep original announcement dates; distinguish source publication dates from event dates and application deadlines.
Do not turn planned systems into operational deployments or a company profile into a release announcement.
Prefer original announcements and artifacts, using press and social posts for discovery and corroboration.
Use original concise summaries and source links, without copying articles or unlicensed images.
There is no item quota.

## Review and publication

Deduplicate by the actual announcement as well as its URL and title.
Resolve uncertain dates, local connections and availability claims before selecting an item.
Use the existing `kind: link` format for external briefs, with relevant existing topic slugs.
Research group labels are not new application categories.
The current importer creates published records, not unpublished drafts, so do not run it for this candidate collection.
Any eventual import or publication requires a separate review and approval.

Prefer technical results with concrete demonstrations or reusable artifacts, preserving both the paper date and later institutional announcement date in provenance.

Also retain [ETH's September 10 IBM quantum-system announcement](https://ethz.ch/en/news-and-events/eth-news/news/2026/09/eth-zurich-to-host-a-new-ibm-quantum-computer.html).
It already appears in earlier research notes but is not in the published catalogue, so it is not a published duplicate.
Suggested headline: **ETH plans an IBM quantum system at CSCS**.
The system is planned for Lugano, with operation expected by year-end and access coordinated by ETH; do not describe it as already running or located in Zurich city.

The ETHEREAL event-vision processor is a specialist alternative with measured results, but had earlier public disclosure and should not be called a newly invented September chip.
The UZH MS study belongs in a separate, carefully reviewed biotech selection: an early ten-person study, not established efficacy or an approved treatment; the paper itself was inaccessible in this pass.
IBM Zurich's Qiskit Fermions is useful older backfill dated August 14, not a fresh August 24 release merely because IBM wrote about it then.

## Source strategy

Use company engineering blogs, official release notes and repositories for products; university announcements linked to original papers or artifacts for research; and organisers' actual call pages for opportunities.
Use Innovation Zurich, Startupticker and public LinkedIn for discovery, then follow the original source.
Startupticker returned access errors in this pass, and LinkedIn coverage was partial; these reports do not claim a complete scan of either platform.
All three reports retain their searched sources, rejected leads and unresolved checks so future passes can extend this work without repeating it.
No production drafts were created, no content was published and no communications were sent.
